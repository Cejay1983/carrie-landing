7 questions to ask before you pick tax software

Ask every one of these out loud, on the call, before you pay. If they dodge any of them, that's your answer.

1

Is it cloud-based?

Ask it like this"Can I log in from any computer or my phone, or does it live on one machine?"

Cloud-based means your returns, your clients, and your progress are in your account, not on one laptop. Laptop dies, you log in somewhere else and keep going. It also means you can work from home, the office, or a client's kitchen table.

Good answer: "Yes. Log in from anywhere, nothing to install, updates happen on their end, and there's a mobile app."
Red flag: "You install it on your computer and download updates yourself." That's you doing IT in February.
2

Does it come with an integrated bank product?

Ask it like this"Are refund transfers and advances built in, and is enrollment included, or is that a separate signup with a separate company?"

Bank products are how clients pay you out of their refund and how they get an advance. When it's integrated, it's inside the return. When it's not, you're juggling a second login, a second approval, and a second support line, right when you don't have time for any of that.

Good answer: "Built in. We help you enroll, and the fees come out of the refund automatically."
Red flag: "You can add a bank product later." Later is a December deadline you'll miss.
3

As the software owner, do I get any rebates or incentives?

Ask it like this"When I hit a certain number of funded bank products or returns, is there a rebate, a volume incentive, or a kickback that comes back to me?"

Some providers pay you back once you cross a volume of funded returns. Some don't. That difference can be real money at the end of the season, and nobody brings it up unless you ask.

Good answer: A specific number. "At X funded bank products you earn Y back." In writing.
Red flag: "We'll talk about that after you sign up."
4

What are the renewal fees, and what's the real total cost?

Ask it like this"What do I pay this year, what do I pay next year to renew, and what's NOT included in that price?"

The first-year price is the ad. The renewal price is the relationship. Ask for both. Then ask what's extra: state returns, e-signature, the client portal, extra users, tech support, transmission fees. Add it all up before you compare anybody.

Good answer: "Here's year one, here's the renewal, here's the full list of what's included." One page.
Red flag: A low price with a lot of "that's an add-on."
5

Is e-filing unlimited, or do I pay per return?

Ask it like this"If I file 300 returns, do I pay the same as if I file 30? Are there transmission or per-return fees, federal or state?"

Per-return fees punish you for growing. Unlimited e-file means your 200th return costs you the same as your first. Ask about state returns separately, because that's where per-return fees like to hide.

Good answer: "Unlimited federal and state e-file, no transmission fees, one price."
Red flag: "There's a small fee per state return." Multiply "small" by your client list.
6

What does support look like in February?

Ask it like this"When a return rejects at 8 PM on a Friday in February, who do I call, how fast do they answer, and is that a person or a ticket?"

Everybody has support in October. The question is who picks up in the middle of the season when everyone's calling at once. Ask about hours, ask if it's a phone number or a chat box, and ask if there's a real person who knows tax, not just the software.

Good answer: "24/7 during the season, phone and chat, and you'll have a direct contact."
Red flag: "Submit a ticket and we'll get back to you within 48 hours." That's two days of a client waiting.
7

Is training included, and will you convert my prior-year data?

Ask it like this"Do you train me on the software before the season, and will you bring my last-year clients over from my old software so I'm not retyping everyone?"

Two things that decide whether January is calm. Training means you're not learning the software on a live return. Data conversion means your returning clients are already in there with their info, and you're updating, not rebuilding.

Good answer: "Training's included, we do it before the season, and we handle the conversion for you."
Red flag: "There are tutorial videos." That's not training, that's homework.
